# C2

Advice for Stanford?
Quals are difficult -- study hard this summer, but it's like muscle memory, so it'll come back. Don't compare yourself with other people. Other people there will be smarter and better than you, and they'll work harder. It is important to view your PhD as a relationship between you and math.
What was your grad school experience? What went well and what would you change if you had to do it again?
I only met with my advisor six times during my PhD. The first time, it was to ask him to be my advisor. I was very stressed and wanted a flexible advisor. He was so flexible that there was a joke around the department: "if you tell [advisor's name] that you're quitting math to go save kids in Africa, then he'll just smile supportively and say, 'Wonderful!'". 

At the same time, my friend, who was in my year, was also my advisor's student. And every time they had a meeting, my friend would first come into my office and run through all of the proofs that he had worked out during the week. It made me feel that I needed to prove something first before meeting with my advisor. That's why I didn't meet with him for two years! He knew I existed and that I was his student, but we didn't meet until I finally proven something small over the summer. So I go to meet with him, and 20 minutes into presenting my proof, he stops me and goes, "You have your thesis". This taught me to be an independent researcher, but I wish I'd met him more -- I feel I could have learned much more from him, and still been independent.
